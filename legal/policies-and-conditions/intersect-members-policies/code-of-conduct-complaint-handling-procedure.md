# Code of Conduct Complaint Handling Procedure

_Approved by Intersect Steering Committee_ on 20th July 2026&#x20;

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this procedure is to provide a fair, consistent, confidential, and transparent process for receiving, assessing, investigating, and resolving complaints about members or staff conduct (as per Intersect’s ByLaws, Membership Agreement and Code of Conduct)

This procedure aims to ensure complaints are handled by the appropriate body, investigated impartially, documented appropriately, and resolved within agreed service levels.

***

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to complaints relating to individuals or groups covered including but not limited to:

* Members
* Associates
* Staff
* Board Members
* Committee Members
* Other individuals acting in an official capacity on behalf of Intersect

Any individual may submit a complaint.

***

## 3. Guiding Principles

Complaints should be handled in accordance with the following principles:

* Fairness – All complaints will be assessed objectively, impartially, and without bias, ensuring all parties have an opportunity to be heard.
* Confidentiality – Information relating to a complaint will only be shared with those who require access to fulfil their responsibilities within the process.
* Consistency – Similar complaints should be handled in a consistent manner, applying the same standards, procedures, and decision-making approach.
* Timeliness – Complaints should be acknowledged, investigated, and resolved within reasonable and agreed timeframes.
* Transparency (where appropriate) – The process should be clear and understandable, while balancing transparency with the need to protect confidential information and personal privacy.
* Respect for all parties – Everyone involved in the complaint process should be treated with dignity, respect, and professionalism throughout the process.
* Proper record keeping – Accurate and complete records of complaints, investigations, decisions, and outcomes should be maintained to support accountability and an appropriate audit trail.
* Compliance with applicable legal requirements – Complaints must be handled in accordance with applicable laws, regulations, and Intersect policies.

***

## 5. Complaint Handling Process

## Foundation

* Email inbox for complaints: [conduct@intersectmbo.org](mailto:conduct@intersectmbo.org)
* Secure space to document complaints and decisions: dedicated private folder
* A review body composed of ISC members and Intersect Staff:
  * 4 ISC elected members
  * Executive Director
  * SLT accountable for Membership and Governance
  * SLT accountable for Ecosystem
  * Lead responsible to manage committees and working groups
  * SLT internally accountable for HR
* Members NDAs signed
* General rules for that body:
  * Conflict of Interest
    * Any COI need to be declared at the beginning of every meeting
    * To ensure impartiality, members of the reviewing body must not participate in matters where they have a conflict of interest.
    * If a complaint is made against an individual member of the reviewing body:
      * that individual must not participate in the discussion, investigation, or decision;
      * the remaining members will continue the process, provided quorum requirements are met.
    * If a complaint concerns the reviewing body as a whole, or where the body cannot reasonably be considered impartial:
      * the complaint will not be handled by that body;
      * responsibility for the complaint will be referred to the Board.
  * The body simply triages, reviews and provides a recommendation on the next step. Any decision that has impact on members or staff will be passed to the Board (or next relevant decision maker)
  * Quorum in the body
    * ISC Members: quorum of at least 2
    * Staff: quorum of at least 3

### Step 1 – Complaint Submission

The official submission channel for Code of Conduct complaints is:

conduct@intersectmbo.org

Complaints may be submitted by any individual.

While complaints should be submitted directly to conduct@intersectmbo.org, they may also be received through other communication channels, including but not limited to:

* email sent to another Intersect email address;
* private messages to Intersect staff, Secretaries, Board Members, Committee Members, or Members;
* other official Intersect communication channels.

To ensure all complaints are managed consistently, any Code of Conduct complaint received outside the official submission channel must be forwarded to conduct@intersectmbo.org as soon as practicable.

Upon receipt, the complaint will be recorded and acknowledged before proceeding to the initial assessment.

Where possible, complaints should include:

* a description of the incident;
* the individuals involved;
* list of violated bylaws;
* relevant dates and locations (where applicable);
* supporting evidence;
* any other information that may assist the investigation.

***

### Step 2 – Initial Assessment

The first triage is performed by Intersect People sector lead (with Ecosystem sector lead and Membership sector lead as backup). This triage will determine:

The responsible body performs an initial review to determine:

* whether the complaint falls within the scope of the Code of Conduct;
  * If yes, within which category
* whether sufficient information has been provided;
* whether additional information is required;
* whether legal advice should be sought before proceeding.
* Any email that is not obviously a genuine complaint will not be assessed&#x20;
* Any complaint that does not breach Code of Conduct might not progress to further steps

Target Service Level

* Initial review completed within 5 business days.

***

### Step 3 – Investigation

If accepted, the responsible body will:

* record the complaint in a dedicated google doc;
* maintain all documentation securely in defined g-drive space;
* gather relevant evidence;
* request additional information where required;
* interview relevant parties where appropriate;
* engage with legal team when the complaint requires it; &#x20;
* maintain confidentiality throughout the investigation.

Members of the reviewing body should have signed appropriate confidentiality agreements (e.g., NDAs).

***

### Step 4 – Assessment

Following the investigation, the reviewing body will assess:

* available evidence;
* seriousness of the conduct;
* previous related behaviour (where relevant);
* impact on affected individuals and the community;
* consistency with previous decisions.

Complaints will be classified according to severity (Low, Medium, or Critical). The severity classification may influence the investigation process, escalation requirements, and potential outcomes.

***

### Step 5 – Recommendation & Decision

The reviewing body discusses the findings and determines an appropriate outcome.

Where a formal decision is required, the body will vote in accordance with its governance procedures and if needed share its recommendation with the Board (or the next relevant decision maker)

***

### Step 6 – Notification

Relevant parties are notified of the outcome.

The reviewing body will determine:

* who should receive notification;
* what information should be communicated;
* whether information should be anonymised to protect privacy.

***

### Step 7 – Record Keeping

The reviewing body will maintain secure records of:

* complaints received;
* supporting evidence;
* investigation materials;
* decisions made;
* communications;
* final outcomes.

All complaints, including those initially received through other communication channels and subsequently forwarded to conduct@intersectmbo.org, should be recorded in a central complaint register to ensure a complete audit trail, consistent case management, and a single source of truth for all Code of Conduct matters.

Records should be retained in accordance with Intersect's record management requirements.

***
